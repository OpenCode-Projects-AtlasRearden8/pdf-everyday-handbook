# Fill In Any Form (Without Paying for Software)

You should never need a paid subscription to fill in a form. Repeat that out loud if you have ever been cornered into a monthly plan by a flashing "UPGRADE TO COMPLETE" banner. The free, mostly-local paths below cover nearly every form you will meet.

## First, know which kind of form you have

- **Interactive form** — it has real answer *fields* (wells waiting for ink). Click a spot and a box highlights. Firefox, Chrome, Adobe Reader, and Preview on Mac can type into these and save the result, all free, all local.
- **Flat form** — a PDF that *looks* like a form but has no fields at all; it is really a photograph or drawing of a form. No software can "fill" it, because there is nothing there to fill. You simply *stamp* your answers on top (next section).

Not sure which you have? Open the PDF and click around on an answer line. A highlight or a caret means interactive. Nothing at all means flat. Twenty seconds, and you now know the right tool.

## Path A: interactive form — use a free local tool

Pick whichever is already installed:

1. **Firefox** — open the form, fill it in, then File, Save. Done. (Firefox is the sneakiest good choice; it even saves typed answers if you need to come back later.)
2. **Chrome or Edge** — fill in, then use the PDF viewer's own Save.
3. **Adobe Acrobat Reader** — the free (Reader) edition fills and saves forms. Only ever install Reader, never the trial of paid Acrobat. If an installer pushes a trial, cancel and reinstall the plain Reader.
4. **Mac Preview** — the Markup tools do text boxes, checkmarks, and a signature from trackpad or a photo of your ink signature, which sounds like a hack but works beautifully on flat forms too.

> Remember: if the tool warns the form "cannot be saved with your data," see the flatten trick below.

## Path B: flat form — stamp your answers on top

You are adding ink to a photograph. Two good ways:

1. **Write on the scan (annotate).** In Preview (Mac) or Firefox/Edge annotations, add a text box for each answer, sized roughly to the printed line. Tedious for big forms; perfect for a few blanks.
2. **Print, write by hand, scan back.** Old but unbeatable for handwriting-style answers, sketches, and initials-in-margin forms. Scanning steps live in the companion printer-and-scanner-handbook. Coming back digital: the result is a *scan*, so it is in the scanned-PDF family — treat it accordingly (chapters 07 and 08).

For asking-someone-else-to-type jobs, LibreOffice Draw (free, desktop) can open many flat PDFs and place text boxes directly on the page; save the result back as PDF with a fixed layout.

## The flatten trick — make your filled answers permanent

Some forms arrive as interactive, but the recipient's software ignores your typed answers when it re-opens the file later. The fix: once the form is filled, **re-save it so the ink is painted onto the page**, not stored in fields.

1. Fill the form in Firefox or Reader.
2. Print it to PDF ("Print, choose Save as PDF" — no paper involved).
3. The printed-to-PDF copy shows every answer as plain ink. *That* is the copy you send.

This is the closest thing PDFs have to "permanent marker." It is also how you guarantee what-you-see is what-they-see, on any viewer.

## When the fields just will not behave

Field boxes refuse to appear? Nine times out of ten the file was flattened by whoever made it, or the fields live on pages after your viewer stopped loading. Try in order:

1. Open in a different viewer (Firefox if you tried Reader, Reader if you tried Firefox).
2. Check whether that page is actually a scan hiding inside a mostly-interactive PDF. Scan pages are flat forever.
3. Give up gracefully and use Path B. Your time is worth more than the elegant solution.

## A privacy word, since we promised one

The big-browser-tool space is full of sites that say "upload your form, fill it online, download it free!" Read that with chapter 01 in mind: you are uploading a document full of your personal data to somebody else's server. Sometimes that trade is worth it (a public, non-personal document, a tool you trust). Often it is not. Everything in this chapter can be done on your own machine. Do that whenever the form contains your name, income, address, license numbers, or anything medical. Local-first is not a slogan; it is a boundary.
